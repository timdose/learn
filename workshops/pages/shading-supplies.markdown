# Intro to Shading

## Supply list

#### Paper

Any good quality drawing paper will work. You want some texture, but not too much. For this workshop, a 9×12 pad will be good, though you could go a little bigger or smaller.

Recommendation: [Strathmore 400, 80lb, medium texture](https://www.dickblick.com/items/strathmore-400-series-drawing-paper-pad-9-x-12-24-sheets/)

#### Pencils

You want a range of pencils from soft to hard. The brand isn’t super important, as long as they’re decent quality artist pencils.

Recommendation: [Staedtler Lumograph HB, H, 2H, 4H](https://www.dickblick.com/products/staedtler-lumograph-drawing-sketching-pencils/)

#### Eraser

We just want a regular kneaded eraser that’s fairly firm. Unfortunately the quality of a lot of brands of kneaded erasers has really dropped lately and they’re super squishy or sticky.

Recommendation: [Blick kneaded eraser](https://www.dickblick.com/products/blick-kneaded-erasers/)

Optional: A pen-style eraser like the [Tombow Mono Zero Round](https://www.dickblick.com/items/tombow-mono-zero-refillable-eraser-round-single/)

#### Sharpener

We’ll be getting our pencils super sharp! Most manual and electric sharpeners don’t get quite sharp enough. Check out the videos on [this page](https://timdose.notion.site/Shading-workshop-851484873f924f9982b045073c83f621) for a few different methods.



#### Board

Any drawing board will do!

#### Easel

I **highly recommend** working upright. It makes it much easier to use lighter pressure to make lighter values.

Any easel will do. If you don’t have an easel, see if you can find a wall or something else upright to work on.
