# Form Zone Vocabulary — Options
*May 2026*

---

## The problem with "halftone"

"Halftone" implies a value level (halfway) rather than a form orientation. It encourages students to look for a specific lightness rather than to observe the angle of the form relative to the light. Goal: replace it with vocabulary that keeps students thinking about the shape of the form.

"Raking light" (from a teacher) is better — it suggests light skimming across a surface at a low angle — but may require explanation.

---

## 3-zone frameworks

| Framework | Zone 1 (most lit) | Zone 2 (middle) | Zone 3 (shadow) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prepositions | Toward | Across | Away |
| Facing family | Facing | Turning | Turned |
| Light-arrival | Hit | Grazing | Missed |

**Toward / Across / Away** — All three are spatial prepositions, one word each, no value connotation. "Across" is particularly strong: it evokes light raking sideways across a surface (the geometry of what's actually happening near the terminator).

**Facing / Turning / Turned** — Uses verb forms to suggest process. "Turning" implies the form is actively rotating away, which keeps students in form-thinking mode. "Turned" implies the rotation is complete (fully in shadow). Has precedent in drawing instruction ("find where the form turns").

**Hit / Grazing / Missed** — Informal and vivid. "Grazing" has the same geometry as raking. Risk: may feel too casual.

---

## 4-zone frameworks

The 3-zone models lump too much into the lit zone. The light-facing point is one of the most important concepts, and it deserves more resolution than "everything that isn't turning/grazing."

Split: [directly facing] + [broadly facing but not direct] + [turning/raking] + [away]

| Framework | Zone 1 (direct) | Zone 2 (broad lit) | Zone 3 (raking) | Zone 4 (shadow) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prepositions | Facing | Toward | Across | Away |
| Facing family | Facing | Open | Turning | Turned |
| Light-arrival | Direct | Indirect | Raking | Shadow |

**Facing / Toward / Across / Away** — Natural spatial progression. All directional, one word each. "Toward" splits the lit zone: *facing* is dead-on, *toward* is still oriented toward the light but not directly. Keeps "across" and "away" from the 3-zone version.

**Facing / Open / Turning / Turned** — "Open" suggests the surface is still open to the light (not closed off). The open/closed axis reinforces form geometry. "Turning/Turned" maintains the form-motion vocabulary.

**Direct / Indirect / Raking / Shadow** — Describes how the light arrives at the surface. "Raking" is the strongest single word for the near-terminator zone.

---

## Key considerations

- Avoid implying a value level (no "half-", no "mid-", no "partial")
- Prefer words that describe direction or orientation, not brightness
- Shorter is better — students need to remember these
- The **4-zone** model is probably better than 3-zone because the facing zone contains the concept students most often miss

## Instinct to develop further

The user's framing: *"facing the light" / "almost facing the light" / "turning" / "turned"* — where "the light" is the understood context, and the three/four terms describe the relationship to it. This suggests a framework where you establish the context once ("relative to the light:") and students learn to classify any surface patch with one word.

**Facing / Toward / Across / Away** fits this pattern most cleanly.
